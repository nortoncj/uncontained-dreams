# UnContained Dreams

Turnkey shipping container homes, built for Southeast Texas. Based around Sugar Land, serving Fort Bend County first.

## Positioning

**For** Fort Bend homeowners and landowners who want a guest house, rental unit, or full-time home without a year-long custom build,
**UnContained Dreams is** the Texas container home builder that delivers a finished, furnished home at a price you can see before you call.
**Unlike** sellers who ship a modified steel box and leave you to sort out the rest, we design it, build it, furnish it, and deliver it.

The category is crowded with Instagram builders, drop-shippers, and outright scams. Trust is the whole game. The brand wins by being the most transparent option in the room, not the flashiest.

### Brand promise

**Steel-built. Texas-ready. Priced in the open.**

### Four pillars (every page should prove at least one)

| Pillar | What it means | Proof on the site |
|---|---|---|
| Priced in the open | Real price ranges on every model page, before a form | The Rising Sun: $85k base, up to $120k fully upgraded |
| Turnkey | Furniture included, delivery included | Custom furniture included with the sale, 1,000 delivery miles included |
| Show the work | Nothing hidden behind renders | 3D tour, build-process photos, spec sheets |
| Texas-ready | Built for heat, humidity, and storms | Insulation, HVAC, and wind specs on every model (confirm with client before publishing) |

### Who we're talking to

1. **The family planner** (primary). Sugar Land / Missouri City homeowner adding a backyard unit for aging parents or adult kids. Cares about permits, HOA, and "will this look cheap next to my house."
2. **The landowner**. Has acreage in Richmond, Rosenberg, Fulshear. Wants a full-time home or weekend place without a stick-built timeline.
3. **The investor**. Short-term rental or long-term rental. Cares about cost per square foot and resale.

## Voice

Plain, confident, contractor-honest. Talk like the builder who answers his phone, not a luxury real estate brochure.

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "The Rising Sun starts at $85,000 with furniture and delivery included." | "Contact us for pricing on your dream home!" |
| "Most Sugar Land HOAs require approval first. Here's what to send them." | "Live your best tiny life!" |
| "Prequalify in a few minutes. Checking won't affect your credit." | "Easy financing for everyone!" |
| Short sentences. Numbers. Specifics. | Exclamation points, "dream" in every headline |

The name already says "dreams." The copy doesn't need to. Let the name be aspirational and the copy be factual.

**Tagline:** retire "Tiny Living Big Luxury" as the headline. "Tiny" undersells a $100k home and "luxury" reads like influencer copy. Lead with **"Container homes, built for Texas."** Keep "luxury" for model-page descriptions where the finishes back it up.

## Visual foundations

**Color.** Harbor navy carries the brand: nav, footer, headings, hero bands. Corten (weathering-steel rust) is the accent, reserved for the one action that matters on each screen. Live oak green marks financing and success. Sand is a warm highlight that only sits on harbor, like a price badge on a dark hero. Pages sit on galvanized grey, not cream and not pure white, so white cards and forms lift off it.

- One corten element per viewport. If two buttons are orange, neither is the primary.
- Harbor for trust surfaces: header, footer, the financing section.
- Live oak only means "good news": approved, included, done.
- Body text is `ink` on `galvanized` or `surface`. Never `corten` text.

**Type.** Archivo for headlines (industrial, built-structure feel). Public Sans for body (designed for the U.S. government, reads as plain and trustworthy). Both on Google Fonts. Headlines in sentence case, never all caps except `label` style.

**Shape.** Hard corners. `radius-sm` (2px) on cards and images, `radius-md` only on buttons, `radius-pill` only on chips and badges. Containers are rectangles; the UI should feel built, not bubbly.

**Imagery.** Real photos of real builds, always. Daylight exteriors on actual Texas lots, interiors with the furniture in place. No stock photos of smiling families, no renders passed off as finished homes. If it's a render, label it.

## Trust signals (non-negotiable on the new site)

- Real phone number and Sugar Land-area address in the header or footer
- Price range on every model page
- Licensing, insurance, and warranty terms on one page (`/how-it-works/`)
- Reviews with names and cities, pulled from Google, not typed into the site
- Financing copy that says who the lender is and that prequal is a soft pull
- Privacy line on every form: "We only ask for contact info. Never your SSN or bank details."
